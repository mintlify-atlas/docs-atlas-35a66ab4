title: "WhatCable: USB-C cable and port diagnostics for macOS"
description: "WhatCable reads IOKit on Apple Silicon Macs to show cable speed, power capacity, e-marker details, and charging bottlenecks in plain English."
---

USB-C cables all look identical in your drawer, but a cable that physically fits your port can be anywhere from a USB 2.0 charge-only trickle to a 240 W / 40 Gbps Thunderbolt 4 link. macOS already knows the difference — it negotiates everything via USB Power Delivery and exposes the result through IOKit — but that data has never had a friendly face. WhatCable puts it in your menu bar and on your terminal, in plain English, so you can see at a glance exactly what each cable can do and why your Mac might be charging slowly.

## What WhatCable shows

WhatCable reads three IOKit service families and translates the raw negotiation data into a per-port card for every USB-C port on your Mac.

**At-a-glance headline status**

Each port card opens with a one-line verdict: *Thunderbolt / USB4*, *USB device*, *Charging only*, *Slow USB / charge-only cable*, or *Nothing connected*. If a charger is attached, the wattage is appended to the headline (e.g. *USB device · 96W charger*).

**Charging diagnostic banner**

When a charger is plugged in, WhatCable identifies the bottleneck:

- *"Cable is limiting charging speed"* — the cable is rated below what the charger can supply
- *"Charging at 30W (charger can do up to 96W)"* — the Mac is deliberately drawing less, typically because the battery is near full
- *"Charging well at 96W"* — cable, charger, and Mac are all matched

**Cable e-marker info**

For cables that carry an e-marker chip, WhatCable reads it directly and reports the cable's advertised speed (USB 2.0, 5 / 10 / 20 / 40 / 80 Gbps), current rating (3 A / 5 A, up to 60 W / 100 W / 240 W), and the chip's vendor ID resolved to a name.

**Charger PDO list**

Every voltage profile the charger advertises — 5 V, 9 V, 12 V, 15 V, 20 V, and any EPR profiles — is listed with wattage. The currently negotiated profile is highlighted in real time as it changes.

**Connected device identity**

The USB Power Delivery Discover Identity response from the port partner is decoded to show the vendor name and product type of whatever is plugged in.

**Active transports**

The link layer is reported as one or more of: USB 2, USB 3 (SuperSpeed), Thunderbolt / USB4, DisplayPort.

**IOKit raw view**

Hold ⌥ while clicking the menu bar icon (or enable the toggle in Settings) to reveal the underlying IOKit properties for every port — the same data engineers would pull from `ioreg`.

## System requirements

WhatCable requires **macOS 14 Sonoma or later** and an **Apple Silicon Mac**.

Intel Macs are not supported. On Intel hardware, USB-C ports are driven by Intel Titan Ridge / JHL9580 Thunderbolt 3 controllers. Apple's IOKit driver for those controllers does not expose USB-PD negotiation state or cable e-marker VDOs through any public accessor, so there is no path to surface the same information on Intel hardware.

## Why WhatCable is not on the App Store

The App Store requires all apps to run inside the App Sandbox. The App Sandbox blocks the IOKit reads that WhatCable depends on to query port state, PDO lists, and e-marker data. WhatCable is distributed outside the sandbox as a Developer ID–signed, Apple-notarised app — which means Gatekeeper accepts it without warnings, but it cannot be shipped through the App Store.
